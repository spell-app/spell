<epic-commit> The <epic-commit> element: one commit that built a phase or fixed an item, its short sha linked.

One commit that built a phase or fixed an item.

  • It draws the commit's short sha (its first 7 characters), linked to GitHub, then the doc's sentence about it.
  • The first of a run also draws a "Commits:" heading.

For anyone reading or writing an epic page's markup, or changing this element.
Its vocabulary (every name it uses): EpicCommit.en.ts.
Its component: EpicCommit.tsx, with its style sheet (.css) and tests beside it.

Its parents:

  • <epic-phase>: its commits, oldest first, after Done (else Goal) and before Files
  • <epic-item>: the commits that fixed or built it, oldest first, last in the item
  • <epic-section kind="log">: the Log, one per commit of the epic, with at and refs, among the review lines by time (epic epic-engine P52); drawn newest first

Its children:

  • prose: what it did, one or two sentences

Its link comes from its own repo (a child repo's commit), else the nearest <epic-page repo>: <repo>/commit/<sha>, opening in one shared GitHub tab.

Name Values Meaning Required
sha the commit's full sha (hex) The commit. Drawn as its first 7 characters. yes
repo a GitHub page (https://github.com/spell-app/spell-parser) The repo the commit is in, when it isn't the page's: a child repo's or a peer's commit links there. no
at a time with its offset (2026-10-29T11:46-04:00) When it went in, git's commit time: drawn first, 2026-10-29 11:46. The Log's. no
refs ids, space-separated (p-commit-log q56) The phases (by lasting id) and items its subject names: a chip each, a phase's showing its number today (P52), landing there. The Log's. no

A slot is where the element's own children show.

Slot Holds
(default) What it did, one or two sentences.

A part is a piece of the drawing a page's CSS can reach.

Part Is
base The commit: its heading (the first of a run only), then its line.
heading Commits: with its icon, over the first of a run of commits.
line Its line: the time (with at), the sha, its sentence, the chips.
time With at: its time, to the minute.
sha The short sha: a link when the page has a repo.
ref With refs: a chip per phase or item it names.
  • Hidden until shown: an item's or a phase's git icon shows its own, and the Log shows every one. They set --epic-commits-display. No page-wide git toggle since epic epic-engine P53: the Log lists every commit.
  • The sha: a small chip on the code-well background, in mono, the link colour. Without a repo it's plain code, in the text's ink.
  • The first of a run carries Commits: and a code-branch icon; the lines sit under the heading's text.
  • With at (a Log entry): a row like a log line's, the code-branch icon in its column, the time muted in mono, then the sha, its sentence and the chips; no heading, a hairline between entries.
  • A chip: a rounded outline in the link colour; one the page no longer has, muted.
  • No state colour, and it doesn't fold.
  • epic commit <epic>#<id> <sha> "<sentence>": lists one, replacing the entry already there for that sha.
  • epic commits <epic> --backfill: every phase and item commit in the doc's git history not listed yet, read from subjects (P3: Name -- summary, <epic> I3: ...). It reads the kit and every child repo, and files a phase commit by the phase's name, not its number alone: a phase renumbered since, or another epic's P3, is left out.
  • The backfill (commits --backfill, epic phase ... done, epic merge) also adds each commit to the Log, with its time and refs; it reads the kit, its child repos and its peers, merge commits left out. The migration log-commits filled every page's Log from its history once.
  • Both write <epic-page repo> from the checkout's origin remote, when it's on GitHub, and a child repo's commit's own repo from that repo's.
  • The converter turned the old phase and item commit lists into it (CardConverter, PhaseConverter).
