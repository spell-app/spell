The Spell Language

 a "card" is a draggable two sided view its "rank" is one of ace, 2 ... 10, jack, queen, king its "suit" is one of clubs, diamonds, hearts or spades its "color" is red if its suit is diamonds or hearts black otherwise it "is a face card" if its rank is jack, queen or king it "is the (rank) of (suit)"

That's a playing card, and it's a program: it compiles, it draws, and it can be dragged. Each quoted line teaches Spell new words, so the next line can say the card is the queen of spades. This page is how to write Spell, a part of the language at a time.

Written for: someone writing their first Spell program, or their fiftieth Checked: 2026-11-21, every example compiled and linted with that day's spell The reference beside it: README-spell, every line the language reads and the JavaScript it becomes
  • A program is a list of what's true. A line names a thing, and the lines indented under it say what it has and does.
  • It grows its own words. A quoted phrase, it "is a face card" if ..., is a new way to talk from the next line on.
  • It doesn't guess. The same line compiles to the same code every time. A line nothing reads is an error where it stands, and spell lint names its file, line and column.
  • A view draws itself. a "card" is a view makes every card its own element on the page, with its own drawing, slots and styles.
  • Waiting is free. An action that pauses makes every caller wait, with no word in the source.
  • It speaks Spanish too: every line here has a Spanish twin (Spell in Spanish).

Save the card above as Card.spell in a folder of its own, beside a project file holding just {}. Then ask Spell how it reads a line about it:

spell parse --in ./game 'the card is a new card whose rank is 7 and suit is hearts print the card is a face card, the card is not a 7, the color of the card' What it printed, the end of it · 7 lines 
... the match tree, and in it:
    expression_suffix › is_a_face_card  is a face card
    expression_suffix › is_a_$rank  is not a 7

export const card = new Card({ rank: 7, suit: "hearts" })
core.console.log(card.isAFaceCard, !card.isARank(7), card.color)
  • is_a_face_card and is_a_$rank are rules nobody wrote: the card's own lines made them, a moment before.
  • The not came free: a phrase that starts with is reads is not too.
  • If you've grown a grammar for a parser generator, that's the part to notice. There, a new phrase is an edit to a grammar file and a rebuild. Here, a program adds rules of its own as it's read.
  • The commands you'll use most:
    • spell lint ./game: every error, one a line, as file, line and column
    • spell test ./game: runs every to test ... (section 12)
    • spell run ./game: compiles it and opens it in your browser
    • spell compile ./game --stdout: prints the JavaScript, and writes nothing
  • The friendliest next step is a whole app, simplest first: Example Apps.

A line with lines indented under it needs no colon: what's under it is its body. Inside a template, a bare line is about the template the first line names.

a "card" is a draggable two sided view, aka "playing card" -- A playing card: its rank and suit, and which way up it is its "score" is a number, starting at -1 group "Drawing" to draw its front <text color={its color}>{its rank} of {its suit}</text>
  • Abilities go before the template: draggable, droppable, selectable and clickable.
  • The first comment inside a declaration describes it. An editor shows it on hover, and the compiled code carries it as a doc comment.
  • A comment is --, on a line of its own. An old // at a line's end still reads, with a warning; spell lint --fix moves it to the line under it.
  • After a comma, the declaring line can say more: a short name (, aka "playing card"), a start (, starting at -1), a default (, with default down).
  • group "Drawing" heads the members under it, as the editor's Inspector shows them.
  • A colon still reads where one is written: older Spell, a game:, compiles the same.
The same facts as sentences

A template can also be written one sentence a line, with no body. Both compile to the same class.

a card is a thing cards have a suit as one of clubs, diamonds, hearts or spades the color of a card is red if its suit is either diamonds or hearts otherwise it is black a card "is a face card" if its rank is one of jack, queen or king
  • The body form is how every example is written today; sentences suit a one-off fact.
  • A file may mix them: a sentence can add to a template another file declares.

A template is a kind of thing. Its properties are stored, or worked out each time they're read.

a "pile" is a viewable list of cards its "layout" is one of stacked or vertical its "value" is 0 if it is empty otherwise the value of its last card solitaire is an app "stock" is a new pile its "score" is a number
  • The built-in kinds: a thing, a list of cards, an app, and the ones that draw: a view, a viewable list, a two sided view, a page.
  • A stored property says its type or its choices: its "rank" is one of ace, 2 ... 10. 2 ... 10 is the nine numbers between.
  • A worked-out one has its values under it, one a line, each with its condition: its "value" is above.
  • A member made new needs no its: "stock" is a new pile is the app's stock, and its id is stock.
  • Choices are a kind of their own: a card's suits make card suit. Another template can have one, its "suit" is a card suit; the list is card suits.
  • Names can be several words. the short rank of a card reads short rank, stopping at the first word like of, is or and.
  • Every file knows every template of its project, so a line may use one declared further down, or in another file.
  • the X and its X: inside an action, the X is the thing the action runs on, its X whatever it is now. the card's name is the name of the card, said the other way.

An alias in quotes is a new phrase, as a rule of its own, from the next line on.

The card's line What later lines can say
it "is a face card" if its rank is jack, queen or king the card is a face card, the card is not a face card
it "is a (rank)" the card is a 7, the card is a queen ...
it "is the (rank) of (suit)" the card is the queen of spade
  • A blank in brackets, (rank), stands for each value of that list.
  • The blank reads the word as you wrote it: (suit) reads spade, (suits) reads spades. Write the one you'll say.
  • An alias reaches every file of its project, and any project that imports it.
  • The catch, and it's a sharp one: a phrase in the other form doesn't fail, it reads as something else. spell lint passes it, and only a test catches it: one more reason to write tests (section 12).
  • Not yet: an alias used above the line that makes it. spell lint says so at the line.

to ... defines an action. Its first line is how you call it, and a or an before a known type is a blank to fill.

to move a card to a pile add the card to the pile to deal for card in the deck move it to the stock pause for 50 msec print "dealt"
  • No brackets needed: to move a card to a pile takes a card and a pile. the before a word keeps it literal: to reset the stock pile.
  • The types choose: with to put a card on a pile and to put a chip on a stack, put the card on the pile calls the card's.
  • An action of a template may be written in any file: Solitaire's to play a card is a method of its card.
  • The last line is the value, when it is one, as in Ruby: no return needed. A step (set, if, print) stays a step.
  • Waiting is free. pause for 50 msec makes deal wait, and everything that calls deal, with no word in the source.
    • The old animation, wait for and await are errors now, and spell lint --fix rewrites them.
    • A program's top level starts its actions and carries on, so a long deal doesn't hold the first draw.
  • Not waiting says so: at the same time runs the lines under it at once, and meanwhile load the scores (or load the scores without waiting) starts it and carries on.
  • What a person starts runs one at a time: a double click on Deal deals once, and three clicks during a deal run the third.
a card belongs to one pile a "discard pile" is a pile it can never take a card its cards are draggable when the card is its last card a "column" is a pile it is droppable when it is empty and the card is a king
  • Positions count from 1, as people count: the first card of the deck, item 3 of the hand, the last card.
  • where filters, and sorted by orders: cards in the pile where it is face up, every message, sorted by its sent, newest first.
  • a card belongs to one pile: moving a card to a pile takes it out of the last one, and the pile of the card is the pile holding it.
  • A list guards what it takes and gives up. move the card to the column asks both piles first, and nothing moves if either says no.
    • it can never take a card, it can never let go of a card: always no
    • it is droppable when ...: what it takes, and its cards can be dropped on it
    • its cards are draggable when ...: what it gives up, and its cards can be dragged off it
    • Asking without moving: the column can take the card, the stock cannot give up the card
    • add, remove and clear never ask: dealing, gathering cards back.

A thing hears an event by a handler, its event on its own line and its steps under it.

a "card" is a two sided view it sends "flipped" with side as a direction on double click turn it over send flipped to it with side = up a "pile" is a viewable list of cards when flipped print "a card turned over" a card belongs to one pile
  • on and when say the same.
  • send goes to ONE thing, then up its chain until a handler takes it: the thing, the list holding it, the app, then the program's own listeners.
    • pass event hands it on up the chain.
    • An event still happening (closing, dropping) can be stopped with cancel event.
    • target is the thing whose handler is running, the original target the thing it was sent to.
  • trigger card-click goes to every listener at once, with no chain.
  • The mouse and keys can be read anywhere: the shift key is down, the mouse is down, the thing under the mouse.
  • while the mouse is down holds while it's true: every property its steps set goes back as the mouse goes up.
Every event a thing hears
Event When
click, double click, right click, center click a click on the thing's view, innermost thing first
long press a finger held still half a second; with no mouse, a right click too
mouse down, mouse up, mouse enter, mouse leave a button pressed or let go; the mouse coming onto the view, or leaving it
mouse still down held a second, moving 4 pixels at most
key down a key pressed, to the focused thing, else the app; key is its name
a card is dragged over it, dragged out of it, dropped on it a drag over a list's view (Dragging and dropping)
when creating a card a thing is made: its creation step, not an event
one a template declares, flipped send flipped: the thing, then up its chain
one the program triggers, card-click trigger card-click: the app, every page, every listener

An action can describe itself, in lines under it before its steps: then menus, keys and buttons know it.

a card to turn it over shortcut meta+T enabled when it is face down undoable as "Turn Card" set its direction to up solitaire is an app its "game" menu has actions "deal", "-", "undo" and "redo" its menu bar is "game menu" to "deal" with title "Deal", icon "play", shortcut "meta+N" print "dealt" to draw it <action "deal" />
  • The lines it may have: shortcut (once per key), enabled when, selected when, undoable, icon, titled, and a new one replaces the last one.
  • meta is command on a Mac, control elsewhere.
  • Its title is its words in Initial Caps: to turn it over is Turn It Over. titled "...", or a quoted head line, says another.
  • Its head line can say it all, its name in quotes: to "deal" with title "Deal", icon "play", shortcut "meta+N".
  • Undo: run any way, undoable as "Turn Card" is one undo step, and the app's undo reads Undo Turn Card.
  • A menu lists actions, "-" a line between groups; its menu bar is puts it in the app's menu bar.
  • A button is <action "deal" />: its title, icon, key and enabled are the action's, live.
  • A right-click menu is written inside its element: <context-menu> holding <action "..." />s and <divider />s.

One line, a "card" is a view, and every card draws as its own element on the page, <solitaire-card> in an app named Solitaire. A React component is a function the page never holds; here each card is an element the page holds, with its own styles and its own error net.

a "scoreboard" is a view its "title" is text its "scores" is a list of numbers to draw it <header>{its title}</header> if its scores is empty <text color="grey">No scores yet</text> otherwise for score in its scores <text>{score}</text>
  • A line of markup IS what it draws, with no return: the lines around it pick which, and a loop draws one each time round.
  • Spell UI's components go bare: <text> is <ui-text>, <stack> is <ui-stack>; </> closes any element.
  • Braces hold Spell: <text color={its color}>.
  • draw the card draws another thing, on its own line or inside markup: <segment>{draw the card}</segment>.
  • A thing has ONE drawing, its to draw it. A change to one card redraws that card only, and a card that fails to draw shows its own "can't draw" while the rest keep drawing.
  • Its look: the project's stylesheet reaches inside every view, so .card .title styles what the card draws.
  • Its tag is the app's name, then the template's, in dashed lowercase words: a stock pile in Solitaire is <solitaire-stock-pile>.

A view can have SLOTS: named drawings it places, which whoever draws it can fill.

a "badge" is a view it has slots "icon" and "label" to draw its icon <icon name="star" /> to draw its label <text>New</text> to draw it <stack direction="horizontal"> {draw its icon} {draw its label} </stack>
  • to draw its icon draws one; draw its icon places it.
  • Where the view is drawn, a slot can be filled from outside: <badge><text in="label">Sale</text></badge>. That badge shows Sale; every other one draws its own.
  • in= is Spell's word for the web's slot=, which works too.
  • A slot's drawing carries its name for CSS, so a page can style it with ::part(label).
  • The old it has parts ... reads as an error naming slots, and spell lint --fix rewrites it.

a "card" is a two sided view gives it a front and a back, so the card says only how each side looks:

a "card" is a two sided view to draw its front <text color={its color}>{its rank} of {its suit}</text> to draw its back <text color="blue">?</text>
  • It gives each card a direction, down or up, starting face down; the words it is face up and turn it over.
  • It draws both sides back to back, turning over in 3D as the direction changes. --spell-turn-perspective sets how deep, --spell-turn-time how long.
  • It's written in Spell itself, and compiled once into Spell's runtime.

A viewable list, a pile of cards, is an element whose children are its items' elements. Its drawing is only the frame around them.

a "pile" is a viewable list of cards to draw it <stack> <children /> </stack> to draw its placeholder <text color="grey">Empty</text>
  • <children /> says where the items show: Spell's word for the web's slot.
  • With no to draw it, the list draws just its items.
  • to draw its placeholder is what it shows while it's empty.
  • to draw each item wraps each item's drawing.
  • A second place, <children name="header" />, is filled by a child written <h3 in="header">.
  • Each item gets --index, from 0, and the list its --count, so CSS can lay the cards out with no code.
  • A thing that moves from one list to another glides there on the page.

Every thing has an id, built in, and each view's element takes it, so the browser's inspector says which thing it draws.

t1 is a new tableau with id "T1" to show the tableaus print the id of tableau T1 print the id of the first tableau a "board" is a view to draw it <stack> <discard-pile "stock" /> <card rank=ace suit=spades><text in="front">Ace</text></card> </stack>
  • By name: tableau T1 is the tableau whose id is T1, written in text as Tableau#T1.
  • By place: the first tableau is the first one made, Tableau#1. Every ordinal works, second to last.
  • One the program hasn't made is nothing, not an error.
  • A tag names its thing by its quoted id, <discard-pile "stock" />. Until the program has it, the tag shows a stand-in, then draws it as soon as it's made.
  • A tag with no id draws a new thing, made from its attributes: <card rank=ace suit=spades>.
  • The element and its thing agree, both ways. The card's properties are the tag's attributes, so turning the card over changes the tag's direction, and setting the attribute turns the card.
  • Two things of one name, as in a game with two decks, are numbered on the page: queen-of-spades, then queen-of-spades-2.

Dragging is opt-in: a template says its things can be dragged, and a list says things can be dropped on it.

a "card" is a draggable two sided view a "pile" is a viewable, droppable list of cards it is droppable when it is empty and the card is a king when a card is dropped move the card to the pile while a card is dragged over it set the hint of the game to "drop it here"
  • The abilities: draggable on the card's line, droppable on the pile's. it is draggable under the line says the same.
  • The list's rules decide: while a card is over a list that would take it, the list glows; a drop it won't take does nothing, and the card slides back.
  • The cards after it come along: a column's face-up card drags with the cards on it.
  • A list's handlers, where the card is already a card:
    • when a card is dragged over it, when a card is dragged out of it
    • when a card is dropped on it: in place of just moving it there
    • while a card is dragged over it: what it sets goes back as the card leaves, or lands
    • when something is dropped on it: any thing
    • when a file is dropped on it: a file from the desktop, on any view
  • A mouse and a finger alike: a finger drags the card, rather than scrolling the page.
 a "task" is a draggable, selectable view its "title" is text to draw it <text>{its title}</text> a "checklist" is a viewable list of tasks its items can be reordered immediately when a task is dropped on it print "moved" todo is an app "checklist" is a new checklist its "picked" is the selection of the checklist
  • selectable: a click selects a task, shift selects a range, command (control) one more. the selection of the checklist is what's selected.
  • its items can be reordered: people drag a list's items to new places in it.
    • A line shows where they'd go; once the pointer rests 2 seconds, they move there.
    • ... after 1.5 seconds waits less, ... immediately not at all.
    • A selected item drags with every other selected one.
    • The items must be draggable themselves.
  • The list's when a task is dropped on it still runs, after the items moved.
  • The Task List example keeps the order you drag its tasks into: Example Apps.

A PAGE FILE is a page written the way it reads: its head, then its body indented under it.

a "counter" is a view its "count" is a number to draw it <button onClick={add 1 to its count}>Clicked {its count} times</button> "counter guide" is a notebook page A button that counts its clicks. ## Try it <example "one counter"> <counter /> </example>
  • The head is the page's quoted name, its flags, then page:
    • notebook lets the editor edit it in place
    • locked keeps it from being edited
  • Its body draws, line by line:
    • a line of prose is Markdown, and a ## heading starts a foldable section
    • markup and <example "..."> are drawn as written
    • <if ...>, <otherwise> and <for ...> pick and repeat what's drawn
    • <!-- ... --> is a comment, anywhere
  • Its indentation ends it: no closing tag.
  • It compiles to what the same page in plain Spell would: a page whose body is its drawing.
  • Pages at addresses, with a site around them: Sites and Mockups.
  • Not yet: spell translate leaves page files as they are.

  • Inside a template, a test starts with a new one of it as its it.
