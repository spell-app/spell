 a "column" is a pile with layout "vertical" -- A column, built down from king to ace, red and black in turn its cards are draggable when the card is face up it is droppable when it is empty and the card is a king or its color is not the color of the card and its value is the value of the card + 1

If you've played Klondike, you just read the rule for a column: a king on an empty one, and after that, one card lower, in the other color. That isn't a comment above the code that checks a move. It IS the check.

The Spell editor comes with example apps, from FizzBuzz up to that solitaire. Each one is a real project: open it, and its app runs beside its text; change a line, and the app follows. This page takes them simplest first, each adding an idea or two to the one before.

Count to 100, saying "fizz" for multiples of 3, "buzz" for multiples of 5, and "fizzbuzz" for both. Here's all of FizzBuzz.spell, its comments left out:

to play fizzbuzz for number from 1 to 100 if the number divided by 15 is an integer: trigger fizzbuzz with number = the number otherwise if the number divided by 3 is an integer: trigger fizz with number = the number otherwise if the number divided by 5 is an integer: trigger buzz with number = the number otherwise trigger plain number with number = the number play fizzbuzz
  • to play fizzbuzz defines a new phrase, and play fizzbuzz says it. for number from 1 to 100 names its loop variable, and later lines call it "the number".
  • The counting prints nothing itself: it says what each number is, as an event, to whoever's listening. Console.spell listens and prints each one, on fizz with number as a number; Explainer.spell listens too, and draws them on a page beside the results.

Type a task and add it, tick it off, delete it, drag the tasks into another order: they're kept on this device, so they're still there next time, with no server and no sign-in. First, what a task IS:

a "task" is a draggable view its "title" is text // What to do its "done" is yes or no its "order" is number // Where it sits in its checklist, from 1 it "is done" if its done is yes it "is active" if it is not done
  • a "task" is a view declares a new template; draggable lets people drag a task to another place in its list.
  • Each its "..." is line gives it a property, with its kind.
  • The aliases are new ways to talk about a task: later lines can say where it is active.
  • A view makes each task an element of its own on the page, <task-list-task>.
  • It draws as a form bound to the task, <form value={it}>: each named field shows its task's property and writes it back as you type or tick. There's no change handler anywhere.

Where it keeps its tasks is two lines, in a file of its own:

my tasks is stored on this device a task lives in my tasks
  • After them, the words you already know reach the device: every task reads them, add task to the tasks keeps a new one, remove it from the tasks throws one away.
  • A task saves itself as it changes: there's no save step.
  • To keep them somewhere else, change the first line: my tasks is a data folder at "tasks", and nothing else changes (Data).

Star Wars reads swapi.py4e.com for the films, characters, planets and ships, and Wikipedia for their pictures. Each server is a line, and its rows' fields, in a file of its own:

star wars is a web server at "https://swapi.py4e.com/api" its id is its "url" its rows are its "results" its next page is its "next" it searches names and titles with "search" check it once a day at 5am keep a copy on this device a "character" is a thing it lives in star wars its "height" is a length in cm or "unknown" its "hair color" is text its "homeworld" is a planet its "films" is a list of films
  • spell place ingest https://swapi.py4e.com/api --into StarWars.api.spell --name "star wars" wrote those lines by looking at the server; spell place update StarWars.api.spell looks again.
  • Its rows link to each other by address. Say what a field links to (its "homeworld" is a planet), and reading it follows the link: the name of its homeworld.
  • Each field finds the server's own spelling by its words: its "hair color" reads hair_color; "172" reads as a number, "unknown" as nothing.
  • What's drawn is in its own file, adding to those types with <extend>: so looking at the server again never touches a line of it.
  • A long list draws what fits, and more as you scroll.
  • It keeps a copy of what it reads on this device: its first load sends 6 requests, and a reload before 5am sends none.
  • The catch: not every name has a Wikipedia page, so about one in five of its 260 names has a picture that's really of it; the rest are an initial on a colour.

Where it is: @spell/examples/StarWars; the same as a site, @spell/examples/StarWarsSite (Sites and Mockups).

Klondike in five files: its cards, its deck and its piles are files of their own, before the game itself, Solitaire.spell. Each file sees the ones before it in the project's list.

The deal is a loop with pauses in it, so you watch it happen:

to "deal the cards" with shortcut "R", icon "sync alternate" clear the score for each card in the deck turn the card face down without waiting for each card in the cards which are not in the stock add the card to the stock without waiting pause for 10 msec shuffle the stock -- each row's first card lands face down, then turns up where it lies for row from 1 to 7 for column from row to 7 add the top card of the stock to item column of the columns pause for 20 msec if column is the same as row turn the last card of (item column of the columns) face up turn over the next card
  • pause for 20 msec lets the page draw before the next line runs. What a person starts runs one at a time: a second Restart during the deal can't deal on top of the first.
  • The action's head line gives it its key and icon: <action "deal the cards" /> in a drawing is its button, and the board's right-click menu lists it with its key.
  • The card is a two sided view: its direction, face up or down, and turning it over come from that built-in template, and it says what its front and its back look like.
  • Each card is its own element, <solitaire-card>; each pile is a viewable list, its own element too, holding its cards' elements.
  • A click on a card plays it, and the game moves it to the first pile that says it can take it.
